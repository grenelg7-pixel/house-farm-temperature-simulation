1

One station at a time

Only the active station's controls are editable. The shared state carries over, so you can set the hour in Station 1, cook in Station 2, then test weather in Station 3 without losing the day.

2

Heat has to travel

The stove is a local source in the kitchen. Warm air pushes through the doorways, so the living room and bedroom warm later and less than the kitchen. The orange arrows show the current gradient.

3

Weather is a step input

Intense sun or rain changes outdoor air immediately. Indoor air, canopy temperature, soil moisture and growth respond with thermal and hydrological lag. Sudden changes are smoothed over roughly two simulated minutes.

4

Station isolation

Only one driver is active at a time. Leaving Kitchen heat switches the stove off; leaving Farm weather clears the weather action. The day, season and stored temperatures still persist. The model is a lumped single-zone approximation: no humidity, wind, shading or HVAC. Values are illustrative, not a building-energy calculation.

Simplified lumped-capacitance model: three indoor zones exchange heat with each other and the outdoors, the stove adds a power-scaled heat load, and solar gain follows the sun. The farm tracks canopy temperature, soil moisture, growth and heat stress. Seeking time re-baselines the house with the stove off.
